The Fragment: The Ghost of a Sentence

A fragment is an incomplete sentence masquerading as a whole one. It might look like a sentence because it starts with a capital letter and ends with a period, but it's missing vital organs! It either lacks a Subject (who did it), a Verb (what happened), or it starts with a connector word (like *because*, *although*) that leaves the thought hanging in limbo.

Anatomy of a Ghost Sentence

❌ Broke Fragment

"Because the campus parking lot was completely full."

Why? Starts with 'Because' but never finishes the thought. What happened next?

✨ Healthy Fix

"Because the campus parking lot was completely full, I was late to my morning lab."

Perfect! Added a complete thought that can stand alone.
⚡

The Run-On: The Unstoppable Train

A run-on happens when two independent sentences are squished together with absolutely no punctuation or helper words to tell the reader's brain where one stops and the next begins. It creates instant mental fatigue!

Anatomy of a Fused Collision

❌ Fused Run-on

"My English draft is due at noon I haven't even started writing."

Why? Two distinct claims ("My draft is due", "I haven't started") crashing into each other.

✨ Healthy Fixes

"My English draft is due at noon. I haven't even started writing." (Use a period!)

"My English draft is due at noon; I haven't even started." (Use a semicolon!)

🩹

The Comma Splice: Weak Punctuation Glue

Comma splices occur when you join two complete sentences with just a comma. Commas are like lightweight plastic tape—they are not strong enough to join two heavy independent clauses together by themselves! To use a comma, you MUST add a coordinator partner (known as the FANBOYS: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*).

Anatomy of a Splice Repair

❌ Splice Tape

"I studied all night, I still felt nervous for the exam."

Why? A comma alone cannot merge these two complete thoughts.

✨ Stronger Fixes

"I studied all night, but I still felt nervous." (Add FANBOYS!)

"I studied all night; however, I still felt nervous." (Semicolon + transition!)
