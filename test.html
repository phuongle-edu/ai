Section I: Multiple Choice

Questions 1 to 5 • 5 Points Each

1

Which of the following correct sequences represents the first four core elements of an MLA 9th edition Works Cited entry?

Recall that MLA uses a structured template of core elements starting with the creator and their work before moving onto where it was found.
2

In MLA Style, what is a "container"?

A container holds the individual item (like an essay, video, or article) and is typically formatted in italics.
3

Which of the following represents the correct format for an MLA parenthetical in-text citation when the author is not mentioned in the signal phrase?

MLA parentheticals avoid commas between the author's last name and the specific page reference, and do not use abbreviations for "page".
4

How should the Works Cited page be formatted according to standard MLA guidelines?

Consider how entries are aligned; subsequent lines of a single source entry must "hang" back so the alphabetized word stands out.
5

How should you format an in-text parenthetical citation for a source that has three or more authors?

MLA relies on an abbreviated Latin phrase meaning "and others" to streamline listings containing multiple creators.

Section III: Fill-in-the-Blank

Questions 8 to 9 • 10 Points Each

8

In MLA style, the descriptive title centered at the top of the bibliography page that contains the list of all consulted academic papers and referenced works is exactly titled: (Do not wrap this text in quotation marks or format it in italics).

This exact phrase is plural because it lists multiple citations. Ensure you capitalize both words properly.
9

When referencing a specific paragraph rather than a page number in an in-text parenthetical citation, MLA requires you to write the abbreviation followed by a space and the number (e.g., Smith [abbreviation] 4).

The abbreviation is three lowercase letters and ends with a period.

Section IV: Analytical Short Essay

Question 10 • 30 Points Total

10

Containers and Nested Citations in MLA 9th Edition

Explain the foundational concept of nested containers in MLA 9th edition. Describe how a single item—such as an episode of a streaming television series or a digitized journal article—can require a two-container template structure. Provide or map out a hypothetical Works Cited example highlighting the transition from Container 1 to Container 2, including expected punctuation.

Instructor Grading Criteria & Exemplar

Key Requirements to Assess (30 Points):

  • Container 1 identification (10 pts): Correctly identifies the direct platform/journal publishing the individual item.
  • Container 2 identification (10 pts): Explains the network, aggregator, or digital database hosting Container 1.
  • Punctuation transition (10 pts): Shows that Container 1 elements conclude with a comma before nesting into Container 2.

Model Exemplar Response:

"In MLA 9th edition, a container refers to the larger work harboring a cited source. A digitized source often has nested containers. For example, in an academic journal article accessed via JSTOR, Container 1 is the original print journal (e.g., Critical Inquiry) holding the article. Container 2 is the online database JSTOR which hosts the journal. Punctuation follows a logical nesting logic: elements within Container 1 end with a comma, which links it seamlessly to Container 2 details (e.g., URL or DOI), concluded with a period.
Example format:
Alpers, Paul. 'Lycidas and Modern Criticism.' ELH, vol. 49, no. 2, 1982, pp. 468-496. JSTOR, www.jstor.org/stable/2872740."
